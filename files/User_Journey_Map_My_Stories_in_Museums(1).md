# User Journey Map: My Stories in Museums

## Context

**Primary Persona:** Ruqi Sun, a college student and frequent museum visitor who values meaningful, human-centered interpretation and wants to preserve her own voice.

**Secondary Reference:** Mr. Wang, a casual cultural explorer who wants a simple, relaxed, and interesting museum experience.

**Scenario:** A visitor enters a museum, discovers an artifact, uses the web app on-site, and later reviews or shares the preserved visit story.

**Evidence Base:** 94 questionnaire responses, 3 interviews, poster research, prototype iteration notes, and the current prototype direction.

## Research Synthesis

Users want museum visits to be meaningful, knowledgeable, and enjoyable. The questionnaire shows that visitors mainly go to museums to feel history, appreciate artifacts, relax, and gain knowledge. At the same time, existing museum digital services often feel too complex, too slow, or too one-way.

The final design direction therefore moves away from heavy AR/social interaction and focuses on lightweight playful learning: artifact scanning, 2D puzzles, clue guessing, concise historical facts, and a private story collection.

## Journey Map

| Stage | Before Visit | Enter Museum | Discover Artifact | Scan & Identify | Playful Exploration | Save My Story | After Visit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **User Goal** | Plan lightly and keep the visit flexible. | Start quickly without downloading another app. | Understand why an artifact is worth attention. | Get reliable information with minimal effort. | Learn through curiosity, challenge, and discovery. | Keep personal feelings without AI rewriting them. | Review, organize, and optionally share the visit. |
| **Actions** | Checks opening time, chooses museum, may preview key exhibits. | Opens the web app from a QR code or URL; sees simple onboarding. | Stops at an eye-catching object; takes photos or reads the label. | Uses camera scan or artifact entry to access object content. | Solves a 2D jigsaw or clue puzzle connected to the artifact story. | Adds a short note, photo, or saved artifact to a personal collection. | Browses collected artifacts and decides whether to share with friends. |
| **Touchpoints** | Portfolio link; museum information; friends. | QR code; welcome screen; no-download web app. | Artifact label; museum space; phone camera. | Scan page; artifact result; concise facts. | Puzzle UI; clue guessing; progress reward. | My Stories; private notes; collection. | Story archive; share option; memory review. |
| **Thoughts** | "I do not want a complicated plan, just a better way to notice important artifacts." | "Please let me start now. I do not want to install or learn a heavy tool." | "This looks interesting, but the label is too dry. What story is behind it?" | "Give me useful facts, but do not pretend to know my personal feelings." | "The puzzle makes me curious. I want the clue to reveal something meaningful." | "This is my memory. I want help organizing it, not help replacing my words." | "Now I can revisit the objects and decide what is worth sharing." |
| **Pain Points** | Too much preparation reduces spontaneity; users may not know which exhibits matter. | Complex onboarding, login friction, slow loading, and privacy worries may cause drop-off. | Labels are knowledge-heavy; casual users lose patience, while deep users want richer context. | Recognition failure or long text breaks the visit rhythm. | Games can feel unrelated, repetitive, or childish if not linked to cultural meaning. | AI-generated diary text may feel inauthentic or intrusive. | Photo sorting is tiring; sharing may feel low-value if the story lacks structure. |
| **Opportunities** | Offer a short "start at museum" mode instead of detailed pre-planning. | Use a browser-based entry, clear privacy cue, and one-tap start. | Show a simple "why this matters" hook before deeper content. | Keep scan feedback visible; provide manual fallback; prioritize concise objective facts. | Connect puzzles to artifact details, historical context, and reward-based discovery. | Let users write their own notes; AI can summarize facts or organize structure only. | Create a clean private archive with optional sharing, not forced social posting. |
| **Success Metrics** | Visitor knows the app exists and understands its value in under 10 seconds. | Start rate; onboarding completion; no-install access success. | Artifact engagement time; number of scans started. | Scan success rate; time to first useful fact; fallback use rate. | Puzzle completion; clue attempts; perceived fun and knowledge gain. | Number of saved artifacts; note completion; privacy trust feedback. | Return visits to archive; voluntary share rate; satisfaction/SUS score. |

## Emotional Curve

| Stage | Emotion | Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| Before Visit | Curious | Medium |
| Enter Museum | Cautious | Low |
| Discover Artifact | Interested | Medium-high |
| Scan & Identify | Uncertain | Medium-low |
| Playful Exploration | Engaged | High |
| Save My Story | Meaningful | High |
| After Visit | Reflective | Medium-high |

**Lowest moments:** onboarding/scan uncertainty and fear of intrusive AI.  
**Peak moments:** solving artifact-related puzzles and keeping a personal memory.

## Design Implications

- Keep the experience lightweight: web-based, fast, clear, and usable inside the museum.
- Use playful interaction as a bridge to knowledge, not as decoration.
- Respect originality: AI should support factual learning and organization, not overwrite personal feelings.
- Support both core and casual users with layered content: short hooks first, deeper context only when requested.

## Course Alignment
