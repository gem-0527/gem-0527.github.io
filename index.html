My Stories in Museums

Project Overview

Project Background

This project designs a lightweight museum interactive web application, aiming to solve the pain points of boring rigid official explanations, dull visiting experience and lack of interesting interactive gameplay during museum visits.


Target Users

Museum visitors, including young students, casual visitors and cultural enthusiasts.


Core Function

Provide museum artifact information browsing, jigsaw puzzle game and situation puzzle (turtle soup) game, together with personal visiting story recording, to enrich the museum visiting experience.

Motivation

Our group selected topic A3 My Stories in Museums under the theme of Digital Heritage & Play. Traditional museum visits usually rely on rigid text descriptions and monotonous official explanations, which make it difficult for visitors, especially young students and casual tourists, to develop real interests in cultural relics. Meanwhile, most existing museum applications focus too much on information display instead of playful and light interactive experiences, and lack a private space for users to record personal thoughts and visiting stories.


Therefore, we design a lightweight web-based interactive system that integrates jigsaw puzzles and situation puzzle games to lower the understanding barrier of cultural heritage. We also provide a one-click visiting story recording function to help users collect their own museum memories. This project follows a user-centered design approach, enhances public engagement with digital heritage, and creates an inclusive, relaxed and interesting visiting experience for diverse visitors.

User Research

Research Method

Field observation, user interviews and questionnaire survey with museum visitors.


Research Findings

• Most visitors feel museum text introductions are lengthy and boring.

• Visitors expect lightweight and interesting interactive games during the visit.

• Puzzle and reasoning games can help users understand cultural relics more vividly.

• Users also need a simple private space to record their visiting feelings.

Design Iteration & Reflection

Stage 1: Initial Idea

Initially planned multiple complex AR and social functions, with complicated interface layout.

→

Stage 2: Design Shift

Simplify functions, focus on user leisure demand, retain only core jigsaw, turtle soup game and record module.

→

Stage 3: Final Direction

We adjusted the design from complicated multi-function to lightweight core experience. Finally we focused on jigsaw puzzle and turtle soup reasoning game, matching museum cultural characteristics, providing users with a relaxed, interesting and non-intrusive visiting interactive experience.

Interview Participants & Iteration Contribution

Early Stage User Research & Persona Building (Ruqi Sun + Mr. Wang)

Ruqi Sun and Mr. Wang participated in one-on-one semi-structured interviews and signed ethical informed consent forms at the beginning of the project. They provided authentic feedback on real museum visiting experience, feelings about traditional official explanations, willingness to try puzzle games, and demand for personal visiting story recording. Their real demands and pain points directly supported the creation of two user personas, guided our initial feature positioning, and helped the team abandon overly complex functional ideas and lock lightweight gamification direction.


Mid-to-Late Stage Usability Testing & Product Iteration (Ruqi Sun + Yongxin Hu + Yuanyuan Peng)

Three official interview participants, Ruqi Sun, Yongxin Hu and Yuanyuan Peng, completed full-process usability testing with signed ethical documents. They experienced relic browsing, jigsaw puzzle game, turtle soup situation game and personal record functions step by step. During the test, they pointed out problems such as small interactive buttons, inconvenient page switching and redundant entries. All feedback was recorded, analysed and applied to prototype optimisation and design iteration. Their operation behaviour and subjective evaluation also provided valid data for calculating the SUS score, verifying the overall usability and rationality of the final web application.


Iteration Value

The whole interview group accompanied the complete design process from early demand exploration, persona modelling, prototype iteration to final usability evaluation. Their real user feedback kept the project adhering to user-centred design, avoided subjective design deviation, and effectively improved the rationality, accessibility and practicality of the museum interactive system.

Overall Project Reflection

In this group coursework, we completed the whole HCI design process including user research, persona establishment, crazy 8 sketch, low/mid/high fidelity prototyping, design iteration and usability testing.


We finally confirmed two core interactive game modules: jigsaw puzzle and turtle soup situation puzzle, which fit the relaxed atmosphere of museum visiting perfectly.


Through team division and cooperation, we learnt user-centred design ideas and complete interface development workflow. The limitation is the number of usability test participants is small, and the game level richness can be further improved. We will optimise interactive logic and game content in future design.
